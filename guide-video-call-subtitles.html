How to Add Translated Subtitles to a Video Call

By Akshay Mahajan · Updated November 2026

Live translated subtitles let two people who do not share a language hold a normal video call. This guide explains how it works in Rosetta, how to set it up in under a minute, and how to get cleaner subtitles.

How it works

When you start a call, your browser connects directly to the other person’s browser (peer-to-peer, using WebRTC). A small service called PeerJS is used only to introduce the two devices to each other. If a direct route is not possible because of a strict network, the encrypted call is relayed through a TURN service called Open Relay. Rosetta does not record calls and has no server that stores them.

Subtitles are built in three steps on each side:

  1. Your browser’s speech recognition turns what you say into text in your language.
  2. That text is translated into the other person’s language.
  3. The translated text appears as a subtitle on their screen, and can optionally be read aloud.

Each person chooses their own language, so a Hindi speaker and an English speaker can each read and hear their own language.

Set it up

  1. Open the video call translator in Chrome or Edge on a laptop or Android phone.
  2. Allow camera and microphone access when the browser asks.
  3. Choose the language you speak and the language you want subtitles in.
  4. Press Start a call and send the room code to the other person by any messaging app.
  5. They open the same page, enter the code and choose their language. Subtitles start when they join.

Tips for accurate subtitles

  • Use headphones. Without them, your speakers’ sound is picked up by your microphone and the recogniser transcribes the other person’s voice as yours, producing echoes and duplicate lines.
  • One person at a time. Overlapping speech is the single biggest source of broken subtitles.
  • Short sentences, short pauses. The recogniser finishes a phrase when you pause. Long run-on speech arrives as one late, crowded subtitle.
  • Stay in one language per sentence. Mixing languages mid-sentence confuses recognition.
  • Spell out names and numbers in the text chat or say them slowly, and confirm them on screen.
  • Good light and a stable connection. Video quality does not affect subtitles, but a poor connection delays them.

Can I add this inside WhatsApp, Zoom, Google Meet or Skype?

Not from a web page. Those apps run on their own platforms and do not let outside websites inject subtitles into a call. What you can do is run the call in Rosetta instead, or keep your existing call and open Rosetta’s conversation mode on a second device to translate what each person says. Some of those apps also offer their own built-in captions or translation in certain plans; check their settings.

What to expect

Subtitles lag a second or two behind speech, because recognition and translation both take time. They are meant to carry the meaning of the conversation, not to be a word-for-word transcript. For a first call, tell the other person you will both speak in short sentences. People adapt within a minute or two.
