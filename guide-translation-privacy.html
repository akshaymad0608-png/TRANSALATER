Is Online Translation Private? What Happens to Your Text, Voice and Video

By Akshay Mahajan · Updated November 2026

“Free and no sign-up” does not mean “nothing leaves your device”. To translate a sentence, the text has to be processed somewhere. This page explains, step by step, what happens in Rosetta, so you can decide what is safe to translate.

This guide summarises Rosetta’s own Privacy Policy. If the two ever differ, the Privacy Policy is the authoritative text.

What Rosetta itself stores

Rosetta has no accounts and no server that stores your text, audio or video. A few preferences are saved in your own browser’s local storage, not on a server: your last chosen languages, light or dark theme, your recent translations list, and whether you dismissed the home-screen prompt. Clearing your browser data or using the Clear button on the recent list removes them.

Where your text goes

Because Rosetta does not do the translating itself, typed or spoken text is sent to a third-party translation service:

  • Google Translate is used first.
  • MyMemory (run by Translated srl) is used as a fallback if the first fails.

Those services receive the text and your IP address, and handle them under their own privacy policies.

Where your voice goes

When you use the microphone, Rosetta relies on your browser’s built-in speech recognition. In Chrome and Edge, the audio is processed by Google or Microsoft respectively to turn it into text. The resulting text then goes through the translation step above. Reading the translation aloud uses voices already on your device.

Where your video call goes

A call connects the two browsers directly. PeerJS (0.peerjs.com) is used only to introduce the two devices to each other. If a direct connection is not possible, the encrypted call is relayed through the Open Relay TURN service. Calls are not recorded. The speech-recognition step for subtitles works as described above.

What not to translate online

Treat any free online translator as a third-party service. As a rule, do not paste text you would not be comfortable emailing to a stranger:

  • Passwords, one-time codes, card and bank details.
  • Government ID numbers, medical records and test results.
  • Unpublished contracts, legal correspondence and anything under a non-disclosure agreement.
  • Other people’s personal details.

A simple safe habit is to replace sensitive specifics with placeholders (“[NAME]”, “[AMOUNT]”) before translating, and put the real values back afterwards.

Ads and cookies

This site shows ads from Google AdSense, which uses cookies to serve ads based on previous visits to this and other sites. You can turn off personalised ads in Google’s Ads Settings. Visitors in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland are asked for consent before personalised ads are shown. Rosetta itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics or tracking scripts.

Quick summary

  • Rosetta does not keep what you translate.
  • The text still passes through Google or MyMemory to be translated.
  • Voice recognition in Chrome and Edge is processed by Google or Microsoft.
  • Video calls are peer-to-peer and not recorded.
  • Keep confidential material out of any free online translator.

Questions? Use the contact page.

A practical privacy checklist

  1. Decide whether the text is sensitive before you paste it.
  2. Replace names, account numbers and addresses with placeholders such as [NAME] and [ACCOUNT].
  3. Translate in shorter pieces rather than uploading an entire document with its context.
  4. Use a private or incognito window on a shared computer, and use the Clear button on the recent list when you finish.
  5. Avoid pasting text from a work email that is under a confidentiality clause.
  6. For anything regulated (health, legal, financial), use a service that has a confidentiality agreement with you.

What the recent list stores

The recent translations list lives in your own browser’s local storage. It is not uploaded to Rosetta. That means anyone with access to your browser profile on that device can see it. On a shared or public computer, clear it before you leave.

Why a third-party engine is involved

Rosetta is a small independent project: it does not train or run its own translation model. It connects to existing engines, which is why the text must leave your browser. The advantage is broad language coverage at no cost. The trade-off is that your text is handled under those providers’ policies as well as ours.

Camera and microphone permissions

Rosetta asks for microphone access for voice input and for both camera and microphone for video calls. You can revoke permission at any time in your browser’s site settings. Calls are not recorded, and permission is only used while the feature is open.

Frequently asked questions

Does Rosetta sell my data?

Rosetta does not collect or store your translations, so there is nothing of that kind to sell. The site shows Google AdSense ads, which have their own cookie rules described above.

Can the other person on a video call see my text?

They see the subtitles of what you say, in their language, and nothing else.

Is the connection encrypted?

The site is served over HTTPS, and video calls use WebRTC, which encrypts media between the two browsers.

Who do I contact with a privacy question?

Use the contact page or the email in the Privacy Policy.
